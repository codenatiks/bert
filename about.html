The story

From one loom to a
quiet obsession

In 1987, Amélia Ferreira bought a second-hand shuttle loom at auction and set it up in the courtyard of her family house. Her first commission was forty percale sheets for a guesthouse in the Douro valley. The guesthouse wrote back a month later with a complaint: their guests kept asking where the sheets came from, and several had tried to leave with them.

That letter is framed above the cutting table today. It also set the house rule — we weave for the person sleeping, not the person shopping. No coatings, no silicone softeners, no thread-count theatre. Just long-staple cotton, honest numbers, and a wash cycle before dispatch so the first night feels like the fortieth.

"A sheet is the only garment you wear for eight hours a day, every day, for years. Treat it like tailoring."
— Amélia Ferreira, founder

Amélia's grandson Tomás now runs the floor. The second-hand loom still works — slowly, in the corner — and every apprentice spends their first week on it. It teaches patience better than any manual. Twelve looms run today, all in the same courtyard building, all within earshot of the kettle.

We remain deliberately small: two hundred sets a week, dyed in batches the size of a bathtub, sewn by eleven people whose initials go on every label. Growth, for us, means getting better at the same fourteen things.

In time

Thirty-nine years, briefly

1987

The courtyard loom

Amélia Ferreira weaves forty percale sheets for a Douro guesthouse. The guests try to steal them. A business plan writes itself.

1994

First export

A crate of sateen ships to a small hotel in Copenhagen. The sateen loom is bought the following spring.

2008

The stonewash accident

A linen batch is over-washed by mistake. The "ruined" cloth is softer than anything we've made. It becomes our signature finish.

2016

Twenty-seven shades

The dye room moves into its own wing. Small-batch colour begins; Oxblood sells out in nine days.

2021

Plastic-free, paper-wrapped

Every order switches to paper and string. OEKO-TEX® certification renewed for the fourth time.

2026

The fourth generation

Tomás Ferreira takes the floor keys. The apprentice week on the old loom remains mandatory.

What we hold to

House rules

I.

Cloth over marketing

The advertising budget is the fabric budget, spent earlier. We would rather overdress the bed than the brochure.

II.

One roof, whole chain

Spinning to sewing under one address, so every promise has a name on it.

III.

Slow dyes

Small vats, matched by eye. A shade retires when it can't be re-mixed exactly.

IV.

Repair, don't replace

Send us torn Nocturne cloth of any age and we'll mend it free — or retire it into cleaning rags and thank you with a discount. Nothing leaves this house as landfill while it can still work.
